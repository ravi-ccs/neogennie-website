AI Readiness Checklist

Start with a focused business outcome, not a pile of tools.

A practical first AI project starts when the owner can describe the outcome, the workflow, the current bottleneck, and the information the AI is allowed to use.

  • Pick one outcome: more qualified leads, faster response, less admin work, or easier knowledge access.
  • List the top customer or staff questions the agent should answer.
  • Identify approved knowledge sources and who will review them.
  • Decide what should route to a human instead of being answered by AI.
  • Confirm what data is required, and avoid collecting information the workflow does not need.

Implementation prep

Bring what you have. Polished documents are not required.

The best preparation is collecting examples from the real business: current messages, service details, policies, scheduling rules, and repeat questions.

  • Services, offers, pricing ranges, service areas, and qualification rules.
  • FAQs, SOPs, policies, scripts, training notes, and examples of good answers.
  • Calendar, form, website, email, spreadsheet, or workflow-tool access needed for the implementation.
  • Escalation rules for sensitive, legal, compliance, pricing, or binding commitments.
  • A review owner who can approve launch content and periodic knowledge updates.

AI Agent Glossary

Plain-English terms for owner-led AI projects.

Use these definitions to make AI conversations less technical and more business-focused.

AI agent
A configured AI workflow that can answer, route, draft, search, or trigger next steps within approved boundaries.
Knowledge base
The approved business information the agent can use, such as FAQs, SOPs, service details, and policies.
Guardrail
A rule that tells the AI what to answer, avoid, or hand off to a human.
Escalation
A handoff to the owner, staff member, or qualified professional when the request is sensitive or outside scope.
Data minimization
Using only the information needed for the workflow, instead of collecting unnecessary sensitive data.
Human review
The owner or team checks answers, boundaries, and workflows before launch and during periodic updates.
